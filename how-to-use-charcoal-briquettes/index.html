How to use and light charcoal briquettes

Lighting briquettes is simple once you know the steps. Follow this guide for a steady fire, and see what to do if they will not burn.

How to light and use briquettes

  1. Set up outdoors. Use a jiko, charcoal stove or grill in a spot with good airflow. Never light briquettes in a closed room.
  2. Stack with gaps. Pile the briquettes loosely so air can move between them. Packing them tightly stops them catching.
  3. Add a fire lighter or kindling. Light it under or between the briquettes. Do not use petrol or other fuel.
  4. Wait for grey ash. The briquettes are ready when they are covered with grey ash. This usually takes around 15 to 30 minutes.
  5. Spread and cook. Spread them under your pot or grill. Use the air vents and the amount of fuel to raise or lower the heat.
  6. Put out safely. Close the vents or remove unburnt pieces and let everything cool completely before you dispose of the ash.

Why are my briquettes not burning?

The usual causes are easy to fix:

  • Not enough air. Open the vents and stack the briquettes loosely.
  • Damp briquettes. Store them in a dry place and keep the bag closed.
  • Too little kindling. Use a bit more, or give it more time, before adding the rest.
  • Too soon. Wait for grey ash before you add the pot.

Using briquettes in a jiko

Briquettes work in a jiko or charcoal stove. Keep the air inlet clear and add a few briquettes at a time. They are made to burn steadily, so you do not need to top up often. To make a bag go further, read how to conserve charcoal.

Frequently asked questions

How long do briquettes take to light?

Usually around 15 to 30 minutes until they are covered with grey ash and ready for cooking. The time depends on airflow, the amount of kindling and how dry they are.

Can I use briquettes in a jiko?

Yes. Stack them loosely, keep the air inlet clear and light them with a fire lighter or kindling.

Can briquettes be reused?

Yes. Unburnt or partly burnt pieces can be reused once they have cooled completely and are dry.

Is it safe to use briquettes indoors?

No. Charcoal gives off carbon monoxide, so use briquettes outdoors or in a well-ventilated space and never in a closed room.
