How to conserve charcoal at home: 10 practical ways

A few simple habits can make a bag of charcoal last much longer. Here are ten practical ways, plus tips for saving gas fuel too.

10 ways to conserve charcoal

  1. Use an efficient jiko. An improved or insulated jiko holds heat better, so you burn less fuel for the same meal.
  2. Prepare before you light. Wash, chop and measure everything first, so the fire is not burning while you wait.
  3. Keep your fuel dry. Damp charcoal and briquettes burn badly and waste fuel. Store them under cover with the bag closed.
  4. Light only what you need. Start with a small amount and add more only if the dish needs it.
  5. Control the air. Open the vents to heat up, then reduce the air once the food is cooking.
  6. Use lids. A tight lid keeps heat in and shortens cooking time.
  7. Pre-soak beans and githeri. Soaked pulses cook much faster, so they need less fuel.
  8. Cook in one go. Use the hot fire for several dishes in a row, such as boiling water after the main meal.
  9. Save unburnt pieces. When you finish, close the vents or remove unburnt pieces. Let them cool completely, then reuse them.
  10. Choose a steady fuel. Briquettes burn evenly and last, and you can buy only the amount you use.

Conserving gas fuel too

Many homes use both charcoal and gas. The same habits help with gas:

  • Prepare ingredients before you turn on the flame.
  • Match the flame to the size of the pot and keep it from licking up the sides.
  • Use a lid and turn the gas off as soon as the food is done.
  • Use gas for quick jobs such as tea, and charcoal or briquettes for long cooking.
  • Close the cylinder valve when you finish, and check for leaks regularly.

Frequently asked questions

How can I conserve charcoal at home?

Keep it dry, light only what you need, prepare food before lighting, use lids, control the air vents and save unburnt pieces to reuse once cooled.

How do I make charcoal last longer?

Use an efficient jiko, control the airflow, use a lid and cook several dishes on the same fire.

Can I reuse unburnt charcoal?

Yes. Close the vents or remove unburnt pieces, let them cool completely, and keep them dry for next time.

How can I conserve gas as well as charcoal?

Prepare ingredients first, use lids, match the flame to the pot, switch off as soon as the food is done, and use gas for quick jobs and charcoal for long ones.
