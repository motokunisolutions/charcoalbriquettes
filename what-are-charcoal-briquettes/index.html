What are charcoal briquettes?

Charcoal briquettes are fuel pressed into a uniform shape from combustible materials. Here is what the word means, what ours are made from, and how to use them safely.

Briquettes meaning

A briquette is a block of fuel made by pressing loose material into a solid, uniform shape. Charcoal briquettes use the same idea: charcoal dust and other combustible materials are compressed so they hold together and burn as one piece.

People use them for cooking in a jiko or charcoal stove, for barbecues and nyama choma, and in restaurant and hotel kitchens.

What are charcoal briquettes made of?

Our briquettes are made from combustible materials such as charcoal dust, wood waste, coconut shells and rice husks. Using these materials turns leftovers into useful fuel, and the bags are marked as made from recycled biomass.

Why people choose briquettes

  • Uniform shape. Every piece is about the same size, so they are easy to measure, stack and store.
  • Steady heat. Similar pieces burn in a similar way, which helps you hold a steady cooking temperature.
  • Low smoke. Moto Kuni Solutions briquettes are made for low smoke and high heat, which is more comfortable for the cook.

Are briquettes safe to use?

Yes, when used properly. Like all charcoal, briquettes give off carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot see or smell. Follow these rules:

  • Use them outdoors or in a well-ventilated space, never in a closed room.
  • Keep children and pets away from a lit jiko or grill.
  • Do not use petrol or other fuel to light them.
  • Let ash and unburnt pieces cool completely before you dispose of them.

Charcoal briquettes are a form of charcoal fuel. They are made by pressing charcoal dust and other biomass into a uniform shape, instead of using loose lumps.

No. Coal briquettes are made from coal, which is a fossil fuel. Our briquettes are made from biomass such as charcoal dust, wood waste, coconut shells and rice husks.
