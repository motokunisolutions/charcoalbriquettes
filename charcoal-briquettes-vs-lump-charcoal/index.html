Briquettes vs lump charcoal: which should you buy?

Briquettes and lump charcoal both cook food well, but they behave differently. Here is how they compare so you can choose the right one.

Quick comparison

BriquettesLump charcoal
Shape and sizeUniformIrregular pieces
BurningSteady and predictableVaries from piece to piece
Measuring and storingEasy to measure and stackHarder to measure and pack
LightingA little slower to catchUsually catches quickly
Best forLong, steady cookingQuick, hot fires

When briquettes are the better choice

Choose briquettes when you want a steady fire for a long time: slow-cooked meals, a busy restaurant grill, or nyama choma for a crowd. Because the pieces match, you can plan how much to use and know what to expect.

When lump charcoal makes sense

Lump charcoal usually lights faster and heats up quickly, so it suits short, hot cooking. The pieces vary in size, so the heat is less even and you may need to top up more often.

Can I mix charcoal and briquettes?

Yes. Many cooks start with a little lump charcoal to get the fire going quickly, then add briquettes to keep the heat steady. Light everything outdoors or in a well-ventilated space.

Briquettes vs firewood

Firewood and logs are bulky, burn unevenly and make more smoke, especially if they are not fully dry. Briquettes are compact and consistent, so they are easier to store and give a cleaner cooking fire. Moto Kuni Solutions also supplies firewood and logs if you need them for other uses.

Frequently asked questions

Are briquettes better than charcoal?

It depends on what you cook. Briquettes give a steadier, more predictable fire, while lump charcoal lights faster and heats up quickly. Many people use briquettes for long cooking and lump charcoal for quick jobs.

Should I use briquettes or charcoal for a BBQ?

For a long barbecue or a big group, briquettes help you keep the heat steady. For a quick, hot sear, lump charcoal can work well. You can also mix them.

Can you mix lump charcoal with briquettes?

Yes. Use lump charcoal to start the fire quickly and briquettes to keep the heat steady.

Are briquettes better than firewood?

For cooking, briquettes are usually cleaner and easier to handle, because they are compact, dry and uniform. Firewood is bulkier and makes more smoke.
